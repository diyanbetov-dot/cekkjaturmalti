MALTESE SPELLCHECKER DIAGNOSTIC REPORT AND REQUEST FOR TECHNICAL REVIEW
=======================================================================

Purpose
-------

This document is intended to be given to another GPT for a careful technical
diagnosis of the current Maltese spellchecker. The requested response should
propose general, maintainable fixes and should not solve the examples through
large collections of whole-word replacements unless a manual exception is
explicitly requested below.

The application currently uses an X/Y/Z/W-style correction pipeline, generated
verb suffixes, article and preposition phrase handling, a grammar rule engine,
candidate evidence, optional corpus scoring, and an optional BERTu reranker.
Several layers can still select or rewrite a correction independently. The
examples below show that correct candidates may be generated but then displaced
by a different layer.


1. NEW RULE FOR l- / 'l / 'l- / 'il / 'il- AMBIGUITY
======================================================

Current problem
---------------

When the input contains a separate "l" or "il" before a noun or adjective, the
application currently tends to provide all of these families:

* l-/il-: the ordinary definite article
* 'l/'il: "to a ..."
* 'l-/'il-: "to the ..."

This is too permissive. For example:

* mar il-bajja
* mar l-bajja
* L-unika operazzjoni
* il-mara
* l-problema

must not receive the "to a/to the" apostrophe alternatives merely because the
writer supplied "l" or "il".

Required new rule
-----------------

The alternatives 'l, 'l-, 'il, and 'il- may be generated from an input l/il
only when the following noun or adjective is governed by the VERB "ta" or the
VERB "ħa", including inflected, suffixed, or negative forms of those verbs.

The rule must identify the underlying verb analysis, not merely a surface
prefix. This matters because:

* "ta" can also be ta' or another non-verbal surface;
* "ħa" can be ambiguous with a particle;
* a suffixed verb may look unlike its base;
* a negative form must still retain its underlying verb identity.

Examples:

* "mar il bajja" -> "mar il-bajja"; no 'il or 'il- choices.
* "mar l bajja" -> "mar l-bajja"; no 'l or 'l- choices.
* "L unika operazzjoni" -> "L-unika operazzjoni"; no apostrophe alternatives.
* "ta l kaxxiera" may expose the relevant ambiguity, including "'l-kaxxiera",
  because verbal "ta" can govern the destination/recipient.
* Equivalent forms of verbal "ħa", and suffixed or negative forms of ta/ħa,
  must be recognized through their morphological record.

Important design requirement:

Article normalization and apostrophe ambiguity generation should be separate.
The article may still be normalized when the following word is unknown, but the
apostrophe alternatives require the specific governing-verb evidence above.


2. SUFFIX-AWARE VERB-CHAIN GUARD
================================

Observed failure:

Input:
    għoġbu jikkuntattjani

Current output:
    għoġbu jikkuntattjaw

Required output:
    għoġbu jikkuntattjani

Required analysis:

* għoġbu = għoġob + u
* jikkuntattjani = jikkuntattja + ni
* The underlying finite verbs are both 3SM.
* The surface għoġbu can coincide with a plain 3P-looking form, but the suffix
  parse and the second verb provide evidence for the 3SM reading.

The grammar engine must compare the person/number of the finite BASES after
separating DO/IDO suffixes. It must not compare only the final surface forms.

Required hard guards:

1. If an exact generated suffix analysis exists, do not discard its suffix.
2. Compare the underlying finite base person, tense, form, and suffix structure.
3. If a surface has both a plain 3P analysis and a 3SM-plus-suffix analysis,
   use the neighboring verb and local syntax to disambiguate.
4. A grammar rewrite must not replace a valid suffixed verb with an unsuffixed
   or differently suffixed paradigm form.
5. Keep the valid rule "marret jixtri" -> "marret tixtri", but only after suffix
   and POS ambiguity guards have passed.

Regression tests:

* għoġbu jikkuntattjani -> unchanged
* basta tfejqu -> unchanged
* marret jixtri -> marret tixtri
* a valid DO, IDO, or DO+IDO form must retain all of its suffixes after grammar
  agreement.


3. OVER-PERMISSIVE VERB-CHAIN GRAMMAR
=====================================

Observed failure:

Input:
    basta tfejqu

Current output:
    basta jfejjaq

Required output:
    basta tfejqu

"basta" can have a verbal dictionary analysis, but in this sentence it is the
prepositional/conjunctive expression "provided that/as long as". The grammar
engine is treating it as a perfect 3SM verb and forcing the following verb into
3SM.

Required general fix:

* Do not run automatic verb-chain agreement when the proposed head is also
  tagged PREP, CONJ, ADVERB, particle, or another common non-verbal category,
  unless stronger syntactic evidence proves the verbal use.
* Exact valid suffixed forms such as tfejqu must be preserved.
* Production rewriting should require a narrowly validated verb-chain pattern,
  not merely two adjacent surfaces that each possess at least one verb record.


4. CURRENT CORPUS ROLE AND WHY IT IS NOT SOLVING CONTEXT
========================================================

Current implementation findings
-------------------------------

1. Corpus scoring is disabled by default:

   SPELLCHECK_CORPUS_SCORING defaults to false.

   The corpus index files are present in Essentials/corpus, but their presence
   alone does not activate scoring. Deployment must explicitly enable it.

2. When candidate evidence is built, corpus annotation is currently called
   without prev_word, next_word, or prev_prev_word.

3. Consequently, even if scoring is enabled:

   * unigram frequency can contribute;
   * previous-word bigram scoring receives no previous word;
   * next-word bigram scoring receives no next word;
   * trigram scoring receives no context;
   * therefore the corpus is not meaningfully contextual in the live candidate
     path.

4. Corpus evidence increases a candidate's confidence, but the final evidence
   rank uses a capped confidence bonus. More importantly, earlier deterministic
   X/Y selections and later grammar rewrites can bypass or overwrite the
   evidence ranking.

5. Absence from the corpus is deliberately neutral rather than a penalty. This
   is safe, but means corpus data cannot reject an implausible candidate unless
   competing candidates reach the same selector and receive useful contextual
   scores.

Real examples the corpus currently fails to resolve:

* "għoġbu jikkuntattjani" should prefer the valid suffixed chain over
  "għoġbu jikkuntattjaw".
* "basta tfejqu" should preserve the PREP/CONJ use of basta.
* "kulljumm" should strongly prefer common "kuljum" over unrelated "kulma".
* "baqa sfigurat" should prefer "baqa' sfigurat" over "baqqa sfigurat".
* "minn fejn" should remain "minn fejn", while "min fejn" should normalize to
  "minn fejn".

What corpus integration should do
---------------------------------

* Receive the corrected previous and next lexical words for every candidate.
* Score the whole local candidate phrase, not only an isolated token.
* Apply contextual evidence only after hard linguistic guards.
* Never override exact morphology or create a candidate.
* Help rank already valid candidates, for example baqa'/baqqa and
  jikkuntattjani/jikkuntattjaw.
* Record diagnostics showing the unigram, left bigram, right bigram, and trigram
  contributions separately.
* Be invoked by the one authoritative final candidate selector.


5. CURRENT BERTU ROLE AND WHY IT IS NOT SOLVING CONTEXT
=======================================================

Current implementation findings
-------------------------------

* A lazy BertuReranker object is instantiated and attached to the spellchecker.
* The helper can score or rerank candidates in a sentence.
* Evaluation scripts call the reranker.
* The production correction path does not currently call score_candidates() or
  rerank().
* Therefore BERTu being "available" does not mean it affects website output.
* It currently has effectively no role in the live examples in this report.

What BERTu should and should not do
----------------------------------

BERTu may be useful as a final tie-breaker after:

1. exact dictionary and generated-suffix validation;
2. hard Maltese spelling and morphology guards;
3. article/preposition structural normalization;
4. POS filtering;
5. corpus n-gram scoring.

It should compare a small bounded list of complete candidate sentences or local
windows. It must not:

* generate spellings;
* override exact valid suffix analyses;
* authorize an impossible article or suffix;
* rewrite a token when the candidate set is not genuinely ambiguous.

Useful BERTu tests:

* għoġbu jikkuntattjani / għoġbu jikkuntattjaw
* basta tfejqu / basta jfejjaq
* baqa' sfigurat / baqqa sfigurat
* kuljum nirringrazzja / kulma nirringrazzja
* minn fejn nieħdu / min fejn nieħdu

Before deployment, measure whether BERTu's quality increase justifies its memory
and latency. It should remain optional behind a true/false configuration flag.

6.1 baqa versus baqqa
---------------------

Input:
    baqa sfigurat

Current output:
    baqqa sfigurat

Current suggestions include:
    baqqa, baqqaha, baqa'

Required behavior:

* baqqa remains a valid possible word.
* Omission of a final apostrophe must be checked before consonant doubling.
* In this context, baqa' must be primary.
* baqqa may remain a secondary suggestion if its meaning is shown.

This should already have been handled by the final-apostrophe priority path.
The failure indicates that a doubled-letter selector is taking precedence over
the final-apostrophe candidate or that the evidence winner is later ignored.


6.2 kulljumm versus kuljum/kulma

Required output:
    kuljum nirringrazzja

The input differs from kuljum through excess doubling. The normalization should
reduce the excess letter structure and produce an exact dictionary form before
phrase or pronoun alternatives such as kulma are considered.

This should already have been fixed by X-stage duplicate normalization and exact
dictionary priority. The failure indicates that a later lexicalized-form or

6.3 niehdu

Required fix:

* The exact dictionary hit obtained through h -> ħ must outrank generated vowel

* A plain h may represent ħ when that change produces an exact valid surface.

6.6 l'ħiellek from lilek
-------------------------

Input:
    lillek

Current primary output:

Bad suggestion:

The primary simplification to lilek is acceptable. l'ħiellek is too remote and

Likely guard requirements:

* Do not introduce ħ when no dedicated h/ħ/għ structural rule supports it.
* Require consonant-anchor compatibility after removing recognized prefixes and
  suffixes.
* Do not display alternatives whose meanings are unavailable or unrelated.

This path requires focused diagnostics showing which generator introduced the
apostrophe and ħ.

8. SUFFIX REPAIR FOR illibsuwha
===============================

Input:
    jerġgħu illibsuwha fuq wiċċu

Intended candidates:
    ilibsuha
    jlibsuha

The final surface depends on the existing contextual i-/j- path.

Required operations:

1. normalize the erroneous initial double l;
2. recognize the underlying verb;
3. normalize -uwha to -uha;
4. retain the DO 3SF suffix;
5. apply the i-/j- surface decision only after the verb and suffix are valid.

In the supplied context, the preceding jerġgħu ends in a vowel, so the existing
i-/j- rules should determine whether jlibsuha is required.

This must be implemented as a general suffix repair for valid verb structures,
not as a whole-word replacement.

Regression families:

* -uwha -> -uha
* equivalent -uw + h-suffix combinations
* the same repair with initial i-/j- alternation
* forms with ordinary single/double consonant correction

13. LIKELY ARCHITECTURAL CAUSES
==============================

The examples collectively suggest these structural problems:

1. Candidate generation and candidate selection are still mixed.
2. A deterministic X/Y branch may choose before corpus/BERTu context exists.
3. Candidate evidence can rank a good form, but live correct_word logic may use
   a different ordering.
4. Grammar can rewrite a valid output after spelling and suffix validation.
5. Grammar compares surface verb records without preserving suffix parses.
6. POS-ambiguous words such as basta can trigger verb-chain rewriting.
7. Article ambiguity is generated from the literal l/il surface without a
   governing-verb condition.
8. Phrase repair sometimes depends too heavily on whether the noun is known.
9. Proper-name capitalization can be lost when a phrase is rebuilt.
10. Corpus context is not passed into the candidate pool.
11. BERTu is not called by the production selector.
12. Some suggestion paths bypass the same hard guards used for corrections.
